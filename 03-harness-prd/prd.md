## Problem & user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno  is an AI product-management copilot for RocketShip. It uses the company’s strategy and constraints to help a PM assess product ideas while leaving decisions with the human.  Juno's task is to turn a rough product idea into a structured PRD and development-ready user stories. As it does, it checks alignment with RocketShip’s priorities, identifies missing requirements, risks, and assumptions, and explains its recommendations.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

## 01 Context · Data Requirements

**Required sources, and exclusions**

Provide the product strategy, roadmap, engineering capacity, and evidence tied to the current decision:
- Strategy: Prioritize trust, reliability, compliance, enterprise adoption, and operational scale. P0 work protects correctness, security, compliance, and mission-critical workflows.
- Roadmap: SAML/SSO for Okta and the reporting export migration are committed Q3 P0 work. Reporting table sharding is planned for Q4 and is required before adding AI workload to that database.
- Customer evidence: CSV exports fail for large reports; Pearson’s admin hit a permissions error while adding a seat; Initech’s expansion depends on reporting reliability at higher volume; Acme requires SSO for Okta.
- Capacity and dependencies: Security & Compliance is committed to SSO this quarter and cannot review new authentication or permission models. Platform & Infra is working on export migration. Data Pipeline has limited slack.
Keep each source and its status visible: customer evidence, committed work, planned work, or an unverified statement.

**How fresh must it be**

Freshness
- When Juno starts a task, it should read the latest available version of each relevant source.
- If a source changes during a task, Juno should refresh it before producing the final answer when feasible.
- Each answer should show which sources informed it and when they were last checked.
- If a source is older than its configured freshness window, Juno should label it potentially stale and explain which conclusions may depend on it.
- A source update should affect the next answer that reads that source. The PRD should define source-specific freshness windows; strategy, roadmap, capacity, and customer signals may change at different rates.
Unreachable sources
- If a relevant source cannot be reached, Juno should say which source is unavailable and when access failed.
- It should continue only if the available context is sufficient, clearly label the answer as incomplete, and identify conclusions that may be affected.
- If the missing source is essential to the decision—such as current capacity for a feasibility recommendation—Juno should pause that part of the assessment and ask for the source or a human confirmation.
- Juno should not present cached content as current. If it uses a cached copy, it should show its timestamp and mark it as potentially stale.
Product requirement
For every answer that depends on external product context, Juno must identify the relevant sources, their freshness, and any unavailable or stale inputs. It must qualify or withhold conclusions when missing or outdated context could materially change the recommendation.

## 02 Tools · System Capabilities

**The verb list**

Juno can synthesize the relevant evidence, identify the customer problem, map it to RocketShip’s priorities, check roadmap dependencies and capacity, and draft a PRD and user stories. It can recommend a priority and explain the evidence behind it.

**Deliberate omissions**

Juno can
- Read the approved strategy, roadmap, capacity notes, and product signals.
- Synthesize evidence, identify conflicts and gaps, and assess alignment with RocketShip’s priorities.
- Draft a PRD and development-ready user stories, with sources, assumptions, and open questions attached.
- Recommend priority and feasibility, explaining the evidence and dependencies behind the recommendation.
Deliberate omission: no direct roadmap editing
Juno cannot create or change roadmap commitments, owners, or delivery dates. Those decisions depend on tradeoffs across teams and customers, and the current sources may be incomplete or stale. Juno can prepare a recommendation, but a PM must review and make the change.
Additional omission: no external customer communication
Juno cannot send messages to customers or confirm product availability, timelines, or commitments. The source material includes examples of features being represented as available or promised before they were scoped. Juno can draft a clarification for a human to review and send.
Product requirement
Juno may read approved product context and create draft analysis and artifacts. It must not change shared product plans or make external commitments. It should identify the human decision required and provide the supporting evidence.

## 03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency

**Turn ceiling and escalation**

Use a bounded sequence:
1. Define the customer problem from the evidence.
2. Check strategy fit and priority.
3. Check roadmap status, capacity, and dependencies.
4. Identify missing information and risks.
5. Draft the PRD and stories, then stop for PM review.
Stop if a key fact needed for scope or feasibility is missing; present it as an open question rather than filling it in.

**Latency and cost target**

Purpose: Juno should feel responsive while keeping multi-step work bounded and its cost predictable. Because each model call may repeat the accumulated context, cost can grow faster than the number of steps alone.
Initial targets
- Quick answers: Return a first response in under 8 seconds for at least 95% of requests.
- Full PRD tasks: Complete the standard bounded workflow in under 60 seconds for at least 95% of tasks, excluding time spent waiting for unavailable sources or human input.
- Cost: Keep a standard PRD task below $0.50 per task at the 95th percentile. Treat this as a launch target to validate against actual model and tool costs.
Loop limits
- Use no more than 4 model calls for a standard PRD task: synthesize, check context, draft, and final review.
- Limit retries to one per failed source or tool call. If it still fails, report the gap and continue only if the missing information is not essential.
- Set a per-task token or spend cap. Stop and return the best supported partial result if the cap is reached.
- Don’t repeat the full source context on every call when a compact, source-linked summary is sufficient.
Product requirement
Juno must stay within the configured call, time, and spend limits. If it cannot finish within those limits, it must stop clearly, identify what remains, and return any reliable work completed so far.

## 04 Memory · Data Requirements

**What persists, at what scope**

Keep the current evidence and draft during the task. Preserve approved product decisions with their sources and status. Track open questions separately, and don’t turn a recommendation or planned item into a commitment.

**Expiry and write rules**

Purpose: Keep Juno’s context current and make corrections authoritative. Memory should support the current PM task without turning old drafts or model guesses into lasting product facts.
Expiry
- Strategy: Use the latest approved version. Recheck its source at each new task; if unavailable, show when the cached copy was last verified.
- Roadmap and capacity: Treat as task-scoped unless refreshed from an approved source. Recheck at each new task because commitments and availability can change.
- Customer signals: Keep the original source and date. Treat as evidence of what was reported at that time, not as a permanently current customer state.
- Drafts, recommendations, assumptions, and open questions: Keep only for the current task unless a human explicitly saves them. Never promote model-generated content to durable product memory by itself.
- Human corrections and decisions: Retain with author and timestamp when saved to an approved source; otherwise apply for the current task and ask whether it should persist.
Write and precedence rules
When information conflicts, use this order:
1. Explicit human correction or decision, if applicable and current.
2. Latest approved source of record for that information type.
3. Other dated source evidence, with its provenance visible.
4. Juno’s prior generated output, which is only a draft and never authoritative.
If two current human decisions conflict, or the source of record is unclear, Juno should flag the conflict and ask the PM rather than choose silently.
Product requirement
Juno must store provenance and timestamps with retained facts, refresh volatile context at the start of a task, and never treat its own generated output as a durable fact without human approval. Human corrections override model output; unresolved conflicts remain visible until a human decides.

## 05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations

**The permission tier**

| Side-effect class | Tier |
|---|---|
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

**Justification**

Juno may analyze and draft. Get PM approval before changing roadmap priority or commitment, assigning work, or communicating product decisions externally. Keep the PM as the final decision-maker.

## 06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement

**The check before output ships**

For each recommendation, show its source and distinguish reported evidence from commitments, plans, and Juno’s inference. Check the proposal against P0 priorities, the Q3 commitments, Q4 sharding dependency, and stated team capacity. Flag unsupported assumptions and let the PM review the PRD and stories before they are treated as decisions.

**Failure behaviour**

When a check fails
- Name the failed check. Say what could not be confirmed, such as source freshness, roadmap status, capacity, or a claim in the draft.
- Show the affected content. Mark which recommendation, priority, feasibility claim, or story depends on the failed check.
- Preserve supported work. Return the parts that passed verification, clearly separated from unverified content. Never silently omit the whole output or quietly present unchecked claims as confirmed.
- Qualify or withhold conclusions. If the failure could materially change a recommendation, label it unverified and don’t present it as a final recommendation. If a missing check is essential, pause that conclusion and ask for the needed source or human decision.
- Give a next step. Tell the PM what would resolve the issue: refresh the source, confirm the fact, or review the draft.
- Keep a trace. Include the source and check status so a reviewer can see what Juno evaluated and what it could not.
Product requirement
When verification fails, Juno must report the failure, identify affected output, and return all useful supported work with clear status labels. It must never silently drop output or imply that an unverified claim passed.
